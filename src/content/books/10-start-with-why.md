## Introduction: why start with why?
- Great leaders inspire people to act — not because they were swayed, but because they were inspired.

## A world that doesn't start with why
- Every course of action starts with a decision. Engineer the outcome you want from the beginning.
- Two ways to influence behavior: manipulate or inspire. Manipulation (price drops, promotions, fear) can win a single event; leadership rallies people for years.

## The golden circle
- **People don't buy WHAT you do, they buy WHY you do it.**
- Ask "WHY did we start doing WHAT we're doing, and what can we do to bring our cause to life?" — not just "what should we do to compete?"
- If a company lacks a clear WHY, the outside world perceives only WHAT it does, and manipulation (price, features, service) becomes the primary currency of differentiation.
- Authenticity is key to sales — it builds trust, which leads to loyalty.
- Explaining the reason behind decisions adds emotional context: "I know it's right" vs. "I think it's right." A balanced decision has rational WHATs supporting the emotional WHY.

## Leaders need a following
- Sharing values and beliefs with others forms trust. **We do better in cultures and places that reflect our own values.**
- **Great companies don't hire skilled people and motivate them — they hire already-motivated people and inspire them.**
- **Average companies give people something to work on. Innovative companies give people something to work toward.**
- **People with a clear sense of WHY are less prone to giving up after failures** — Edison: "I didn't find a way to make a lightbulb, I found a thousand ways how not to make one."
- Businesses should find early adopters who share their beliefs, rather than selling to the majority — early adopters spread the word and encourage others to follow.

## How to rally those who believe
- **Charisma comes from clarity of WHY and absolute conviction in an ideal bigger than oneself — not energy. Energy can excite; only charisma can inspire.**
- Reaching billion-dollar status requires a rare partnership between one who knows WHY and those who know HOW.
- **A WHY provides the clear filter for decision-making.**
- Toyota built Lexus as a separate brand for luxury, understanding the market wouldn't pay extra for a luxury Toyota-branded car — Lexus represents the same values in a different way.

## The biggest challenge is success
- Achievement (tangible, measurable) and success (a feeling or state of being) are not the same.
- **A WHY without the HOWs — passion without structure — has a high probability of failure.**
- The "school bus test": can an organization thrive without its founder? Prepare for the inevitable departure and keep the founding vision alive.
- **When people know WHY you do WHAT you do, they credit you for everything that proves WHY. When unclear, WHAT you do has no context.**

## Discover why
- **When you compete against everyone else, no one wants to help you. When you compete against yourself, everyone wants to help you.**
- Organizations start with WHY, but only the great ones maintain it over time.
