## Intro
- Obtaining power needs you to be thoughtful, strategic, resilient, alert, and willing to fight when necessary.
- The ability to effectively self-present is why successful individuals reach high levels.
- Get over yourself and get beyond your concerns with self-image and the perception others have of you — others aren't worrying or thinking about you that much anyway.

## Ch.1 It takes more than performance
- If you're going to create a path to power, you need to lose the idea that performance by itself is enough.
- Relationship with those in power is critical to your own success.
- It is performance coupled with political skill that makes you rise through the ranks.
- **People higher up are too busy — you need to ensure they know what you are accomplishing. Get noticed! Tell them.**
- **Exposure effect: people like what they remember. For your great performance to be appreciated, it needs to be visible.**
- Familiarity produces preference — being memorable equals getting picked.

## Ch.2 The personal qualities that bring influence
- In an interview, project self-confidence and self-assurance. Convey that you have some level of power in the room.
- Focusing on what you need to change to accomplish future goals can be much more uplifting than reviewing past setbacks/weaknesses.
- Seven important personal qualities that build power, across two dimensions:
  - **Will** — the drive to take on big challenges
    1. *Ambition* — helps overcome the temptation to give up
    2. *Energy* — long hours of hard work help you master subject matter faster
    3. *Focus* — concentrate on what matters most
  - **Skills** — the capabilities required to turn ambition into accomplishment
    4. *Self-knowledge* — reflect on what you've done well and what you could improve
    5. *Confidence* — observers associate confident behavior with actually having power. If you aren't confident about what you deserve, you'll be reluctant to ask or push.
    6. *Empathy with others*
    7. *Capacity to tolerate conflict*

## Ch.3 Choosing where to start
- Power varies across departments. Where you begin your career affects your rate of progress as well as how far you go.

## Ch.4 Getting in: standing out and breaking some rules
- Launching your career requires developing both the ability and the willingness to ask for things, and learning to stand out.
- One technique to guarantee access to the top: ask for dinner with the head once a year.
- You need to get over the idea that you need to be liked by everybody. If you don't put yourself forward, who will?
- Asking often works — people underestimate the chances of others offering help.
- **For someone to hire or promote you, they must notice you.** Build your personal brand and promote yourself.
- Likability is overrated — to appear competent, it helps to seem a little tough. Likability can create power, but power almost certainly creates likability.
- Attitudes follow behavior — act in a certain way and, over time, your attitudes follow.

## Ch.5 Making something out of nothing: creating resources
- Getting control of resources is an important step on your path to power.
- Building a power base is a process of accumulating leverage and resource control little by little over time — see (or create) opportunities others may miss, with patience and persistence to follow through.
- Most people like to talk about themselves. Being a good listener and asking questions is a simple but effective way to use a resource everyone has — time and attention — to build power.

## Ch.6 Building efficient and effective social networks
- An optimal networking strategy uses weak ties — know a lot of different people from different circles, with multiple organizational affiliations across industries.
- One source of your power is control over the flow of information — centrality matters, and one way to build it is through physical location.
- Occupying brokerage positions — filling structural holes — is advantageous for a career.
- A large network of weak ties is good for innovation and locating information; a small network of strong ties is better for exploiting existing knowledge and transferring tacit skills.

## Ch.7 Acting and speaking with power
- Differences in the ability to convey power through how we talk, appear, and act matter in everyday interactions.
- In interviews, the impressions people make as they talk matter for job offers and promotions. **Speak articulately, look people in the eyes, be confident in the stories you tell.**
- **Authority is 20% given, 80% taken. If you want power, you need to project confidence.**
- Emotions and behaviors are self-reinforcing — smile, and you're more likely to feel happy.
- Persuasive language: pause for emphasis; use a list of three items; use contrastive "us vs. them" pairs.
- We often avoid situations that make us uncomfortable, but there's no substitute for experience — seek out presentations and talks, and find someone to give you feedback.

## Ch.8 Building a reputation: perception is reality
- You only get one chance to make a first impression.
- People don't pay as close attention to later information as to information that comes early. Once an impression forms, inconsistent information gets disregarded.
- Have others promote you and attract the kind of media coverage and image that builds your power base.

## Ch.9 Overcoming opposition and setbacks
- Move first — seize the initiative. Moving quickly can catch opponents off guard.
- **Act as if — project power and success even under bad circumstances.**
- People want to associate with winners. Show enough strength and resilience that allies believe their efforts won't be wasted — acting as if you'll win becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy.

## Ch.10 The price of power
- You can't have it all — the quest for power entails trade-offs, including in personal life.
- The higher you rise, the more people will want your job.

## Ch.11 How and why people lose power
- When you're powerful and successful, you can become overconfident and less observant.
- You can't always control how much power you maintain, but you can leave your position with dignity and influence your legacy.

## Ch.12 Power dynamics: good for organizations, good for you?
- You need to take care of yourself if you are going to survive and succeed in places where, if you don't look out for yourself, no one else is going to.

## Ch.13 It's easier than you think
- People give away their power by not trying — not trying protects self-esteem but guarantees failure.
- When we stop thinking of ourselves as powerless victims, our chances of success increase dramatically.
- People align with who they think is going to win — if you don't stand up for yourself, few will be on your side.
- **Seek power as if your life depends on it. Because it does.**
