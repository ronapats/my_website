## Introduction
- **"Yesterday is gone. Tomorrow has not yet come. We have only today. Let us begin."**

## How mindful are you?
- Children are great role models for being in the moment — when a child plays, he isn't thinking about yesterday or later.

## Part One: Understanding Mindfulness

### 1. Why be mindful?
- Child → cry → let it go → forget.

### 2. Moving towards mindful thinking
- **Focusing your happiness on something in the future means you miss out on happiness now.**
- Sunk cost trap — refusing to let go means allowing the past to dictate the present.
- **Beginner's mind helps you see things in a new light** — put aside past beliefs, notice new things in familiar situations. You become mindless once you think you already know something.
- **Beginner's mind can transform how you experience life — it keeps you young and eager to learn.**
- "If you really want to do something, you'll find a way. If you don't, you'll find an excuse."

### 3. Mindful thinking and feeling
- The more aware you are of your emotions, the more you can move out of mind traps.
- **Emotions are temporary and short-lived. Being aware of an emotion and what it's telling you is being mindful.**
- Accepting an emotion means letting it be there without trying to change the feeling — it is what it is.

### 4. Mindful doing
- Managing your breathing — the physical aspect — also manages your thoughts and feelings.
- The more often you do something, the more automatic it becomes.
- Slow down; one thing at a time. "When walking, walk. When eating, eat."
- Flow = time spent doing something that keeps you focused and engaged.
- Gratitude can release you from attachment to past failures and future wants.

## Part Two: Putting It Into Practice

### 5. Managing painful emotions: anger, worry and anxiety, guilt
- **Whatever the present moment contains, accept it as if you had chosen it. Work with it, not against it.**
- Acceptance is recognizing what's happened cannot be changed — then you can move forward.
- **Recognize that worrying and problem-solving are different. Problem-solving evaluates a situation and puts a plan into action, now, in the present.**
- Affirmative phrase: "This too shall pass."
- Self-forgiveness lets you focus on positive action in the present. **You are human — you will make mistakes. Learn from it.**

### 6. Mindfulness for self-esteem: confidence, self-esteem, and loneliness
- Confidence isn't about what you can or can't do — it's what you believe you can or can't do.
- **When you make a mistake, accept you can't change the past; know you can influence what happens from now on.**
- **Focus on the positive aspects of life — the things that make you feel good about yourself.**

### 7. Mindful motivation: goals and willpower
- Focus energy on the process — the steps you take — rather than the goal itself.
- **We have a limited amount of willpower. Use it effectively and mindfully.**

### 8. Mindfulness with others: listening, criticism, forgiving, persuading, motivating
- You can't please everyone. Accept it and let it go — don't sweat the small stuff.
- **Three gates before you speak: Is it necessary? Is it kind? Is it true?**
- **Forgiveness is for you, not the other person.**
- To persuade someone, an unusual request catches interest better than a typical, routine one.

### 9. Mindfulness at work: interviews, meetings, presentations
- Be well prepared. Use positive self-talk ("I can do this") and breathe.
