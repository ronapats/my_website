## 1. All humans are entrepreneurs
- The Start-up of You mindset: Permanent Beta (Amazon's "Day 1").
- **We are all works in progress. Acknowledge that you have bugs, that there's new development to do on yourself, that you will need to adapt and evolve.**

## 2. Develop a competitive advantage
- If you try to be the best at everything, you'll be the best at nothing.
- Three pieces inform your direction and competitive advantage:
  - **Your assets** — say specifically what you're able to do because of your experience, not just "I have X years."
  - **Your aspirations and values** — a compelling vision is part of competitive advantage (Google: "organize the world's information"). Work you care about lets you work harder and better.
  - **The market realities** — put yourself in a position to ride the waves; work in a market with natural momentum.
- Place yourself in a market niche where your existing assets shine brighter than the competition's — pick a hill with less competition.

## 3. Plan to adapt
- **The successful ones are flexibly persistent — they stay true to their values and vision, yet remain flexible enough to adapt.**
- You don't know the best plan until you try — actions (not plans) generate lessons that test your hypothesis against reality.
- Plan ABZ: Plan A (aim), Plan B (pivot as you learn), Plan Z (fallback option).

## 4. It takes a network
- No matter how brilliant you are, if you're playing solo, you'll always lose to a team.
- The fastest way to change yourself is to hang out with people who are already the way you want to be.
- Relationship builders prioritize high-quality relationships over a large number of connections.
- Empathize and help first — think about how you can help and collaborate.
- There's a limit to the relationships you can maintain — think of a digital camera: low-res photos in the hundreds, or high-res in the forties.
- When you want an introduction through your network, present a compelling reason it makes sense.
- The best professional network is both narrow/deep (strong connections) and bridge/shallow (bridge ties).

## 5. Pursue breakout opportunities
- To accomplish something significant, focus on finding and capitalizing on great career opportunities.
- See opportunities where others see problems — ask "why?" and "is there a better way?" **When your eyes are open and mind is curious, you can dramatically increase your opportunity flow.**
- **You won't encounter accidental good fortune lying in bed. When you do something, you stir the pot and introduce the possibility that random ideas, people, and places will collide into new opportunities.**
- Get resourceful or die — when you have no choice but to fight, you fight hard.

## 6. Take intelligent risks
- **It's probably not as risky as you think — people tend to overrate risk (negativity bias). Remind yourself the downside is probably not as bad as it seems.**
- Pursue opportunities with lower risk than your peers think, but still high-reward.
- If you don't find risk, risk will find you.
- Pretending you can avoid risk causes you to miss opportunities that can change your life.

## 7. Who you know is what you know
- Asking a lot of good questions is the secret to network intelligence.
- Good synthesis is what makes the whole worth more than the sum of the parts.
