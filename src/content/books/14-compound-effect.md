## Ch.1 The Compound Effect in action
- "It doesn't matter how smart you are — you need to make up in hard work what you lack in experience, skill, or ability. If your competitor is smarter, work three or four times as hard. You can still beat them."
- **Small, Smart Choices + Consistency + Time = Radical Difference.** The "overnight success" is really the result of small, smart choices completed consistently over time.

## Ch.2 Choices
- **Everything in your life exists because you first made a choice. Choices are at the root of every result. Each choice starts a behavior that becomes a habit.**
- You make your choices, then your choices make you.
- Take 100% responsibility for everything you experience — own all your choices and all your responses. "The day you graduate from childhood to adulthood is the day you take full responsibility for your life."
- Track every action related to the area you want to improve — you cannot manage or improve what you don't measure.
- Assess purchases from a future-value standpoint: is that fifty-dollar gadget worth $250 less in the bank later?
- What's your why? Your reason must be fantastically motivating to you, or you'll give up too easily.
- The plank-between-buildings example: the same $20 task becomes undoable when risk feels real — but you'd do it instantly to save your child. **When the reason is big enough, you'll perform almost any how.**
- When faced with a choice, ask: "Does this align with my core values?" If yes, do it; if not, don't look back.
- Start tracking at least one behavior in one area you'd like to change.

## Ch.3 Habits
- Our need for immediate gratification enslaves us to bad habits — it's time for why-power.
- Your habits are learned, so they can be unlearned — make your why-power strong enough to overwhelm the urge for instant gratification.
- Stay aware — pay attention to a habit at least once a day and you're far more likely to succeed.
- **Successful people hate doing the same hard things as everyone else. The difference is they do them anyway.**
- "The ultimate measure of a man is not where he stands in moments of comfort, but where he stands at times of challenge." — Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.

## Ch.4 Momentum
- High-achievers have developed consistent routines for their daily disciplines — a routine built on good habits separates the most successful from everyone else.
- **Review your top 3 MVPs (Most Valuable Priorities) each day: if I only did three things today, what would move me closer to my big goals?**
- You set yourself up for failure by doing too much too soon — it's easier to maintain a regular pace than to take off fast and burn out.
- **Winning the race is all about pace. Be the tortoise.**

## Ch.5 Influences
- Turn your car into a mobile classroom — listening to instructional material while driving compounds into real learning over a year.
- **You get in life what you tolerate.** Life organizes around the standards you set for yourself.

## Ch.6 Acceleration
- "Don't wish it were easier; wish you were better." (Jim Rohn) — hitting the wall is where you find your more powerful self.
- Viewing yourself as your toughest competitor multiplies results — go beyond what's expected.
- Find the "WOW" — a little extra effort that doesn't cost much more but multiplies results many times over.
- Do better than expected: find the line of expectation, then exceed it.

## Conclusion
- Ask: are you where you thought you'd be five years ago? If not, why? Simple choices. Choose to change your life, once and for all.
- Holding true to your why-power with consistent, positive action makes momentum carry you forward.
