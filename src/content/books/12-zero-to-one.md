## 3. All happy companies are different
- Entrepreneurs are always biased to understate the scale of competition — the biggest mistake is describing your market so narrowly that you dominate it by definition. A British restaurant in Palo Alto "owns" the British-food market, but the real market might be the Palo Alto restaurant market overall.

## 5. Last mover advantage
- The value of a business today is the sum of all the money it will make in the future.
- **A proprietary technology must be at least 10x better than its closest substitute in some important dimension to lead to a real monopolistic advantage** — PayPal made buying/selling on eBay 10x better; Amazon offered 10x as many books as any bookstore.
- Characteristics of monopoly: proprietary technology, network effects, economies of scale, branding.
- **Start small and monopolize** — a startup should start with a very small market. Talk of "getting 1% of a $100B market" is a red flag: large markets either lack a good starting point or invite competition.
- **Scaling up: once you dominate a niche, expand into related, slightly broader markets.** Amazon's founding vision was all of online retail, but it deliberately started with books.

## 8. Secrets
- **We will never learn the world's remaining secrets unless we demand to know them and force ourselves to look.**

## 9. Foundations
- To anticipate misalignment in a company, distinguish: **Ownership** (who legally owns the equity?), **Possession** (who runs the company day-to-day?), **Control** (who formally governs its affairs?).
- Ownership is allocated among founders, employees, investors; management/employees hold possession; a board of directors exercises control.

## 12. Man and machine
- Don't ask what problems can be solved with computers alone — ask how computers can solve hard problems.

## 13. Seeing green
A great business plan must address:
1. **The Engineering Question** — can you create breakthrough technology instead of incremental improvements? Aim for 10x better, since incremental improvements often mean no real improvement for the end user.
2. **The Timing Question** — is now the right time?
3. **The Monopoly Question** — are you starting with a big share of a small market?
4. **The People Question** — do you have the right team?
5. **The Distribution Question** — can you deliver, not just create, your product?
6. **The Durability Question** — will your position be defensible 10-20 years out?
7. **The Secret Question** — have you identified a unique opportunity others don't see?
