**Be hard on the problem, soft on the people.**

## 1. The problem — don't bargain over positions
Principled negotiation:
- **People** — separate the people from the problem
- **Interests** — focus on interests, not positions
- **Options** — invent multiple options looking for mutual gains before deciding
- **Criteria** — insist the result be based on some objective standard

## 2. The method — separate the people from the problem
People problems: perception, emotion, and communication.
- **Perception** — put yourself in their shoes. **The ability to see the situation as the other side sees it is one of the most important skills a negotiator can possess.**
- **Emotion** — recognize and understand emotions, theirs and yours. Make emotions explicit and acknowledge them.
- **Communication** — listen actively and acknowledge what's said; speak to be understood; speak for a purpose.

## 3. The method — focus on interests, not positions
- Interest defines the problem. To identify interests, ask WHY / WHY NOT?
- Communicate your interest — be specific.
- Acknowledge their interests as part of the problem: "As I understand it, your interests are… Do you have any other important interests?"
- Be concrete but flexible — successful negotiation requires being both firm and open.

## 4. The method — invent options for mutual gain
- Generate many options before selecting — invent first, decide later.
- Ask for their preference — look for items of low cost to you and high benefit to them.

## 5. The method — insist on using objective criteria
- Commit to reaching a solution based on principle, not pressure.
- Fair procedure — one cuts, the other chooses.
- **Never yield to pressure.** Use principle rather than positional bargaining.

## 6. Yes, but… what if they are more powerful?
- The cost of using a bottom line is arbitrary and too rigid — it can keep you from inventing and agreeing to a solution it would be wise to accept.
- **Develop your BATNA** — best alternative to a negotiated agreement.
- **The better your BATNA, the greater your power.**

## 7. Yes, but… what if they won't play?
- Use negotiation jujitsu — don't push back, refuse to react. Sidestep the attack, deflect it against the problem, don't attack their position.
- Ask questions and pause — silence is one of your best weapons.
- One-text procedure — a great help for two-party negotiation involving a mediator.
- "Please correct me if I'm wrong" — being open to correction is a pillar of principled negotiation.
- "Let me get back to you" — a good negotiator rarely makes an important decision on the spot.

## 8. Yes, but… what if they use dirty tricks?
- Taming the hard bargainer.

## 9. Conclusion
- Whether it makes sense to negotiate depends on how satisfactory your BATNA is and the other side's.
- **Think about closure from the beginning — envision what a successful negotiation looks like, then work backward.**
- **Never negotiate out of fear, never fear to negotiate.**
- Good listening increases your negotiation power by increasing information about the other side's interests.
- One of the greatest powers you have is to reframe — change the focus from positional bargaining to principled negotiation.
- **Find an approach that both makes sense and is comfortable to you — you maximize negotiation power if you believe what you say and say what you believe.**
